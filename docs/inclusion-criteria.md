# Inclusion Criteria

## Required for main-list inclusion

A project must meet all of the following:

1. **Relevant scope.** It defines a programming language, DSL, embedded language, or programming model for AI authorship, LLM interaction, agent workflows, verification, probabilistic inference, or AI compute.
2. **Primary evidence.** An official repository, official documentation site, or primary academic publication explains the project and supports the catalog description.
3. **Identifiable artifact.** Readers can inspect source, a specification, a research artifact, or an executable implementation. Pure announcements and name-only references are deferred.
4. **Distinct contribution.** The project has a language-level idea beyond wrapping a single model API.
5. **Neutral description.** The entry can be summarized accurately without relying on unsupported marketing claims.

Maturity is not a universal gate. Research artifacts and thought experiments may be included in the experimental section when their status is explicit and their ideas are useful to the field.

## Adjacent-tooling inclusion

Frameworks and runtimes may be listed only when they materially influence AI-native programming practice or clarify the boundary between a language and a framework. This section is intentionally selective.

## Reasons to defer or exclude

- No canonical project or primary source can be identified.
- The name maps to multiple unrelated projects and authorship cannot be resolved.
- The only artifact is an announcement, inaccessible demo, or unsourced secondary mention.
- The project is a conventional AI agent, library, or application without a language-like abstraction.
- The repository is a fork or duplicate of an already-listed canonical project.
- Claims in available material are too ambiguous to write a neutral, verifiable entry.

Deferred candidates stay in [research notes](research-notes.md) with the reason and evidence checked. They can be reconsidered when better primary evidence appears.

## Verification policy

- Prefer canonical repositories and official documentation over aggregators.
- Record exact evidence URLs and the date checked.
- Confirm archival status and license from repository metadata and the license text where possible.
- Use `null` rather than guessing.
- Recheck volatile status before material editorial changes.
- Treat external-link failures caused by rate limits, authentication, or bot protection as inconclusive.

## Removal policy

Archival alone does not require removal when a project remains historically useful. Remove or defer entries that become unverifiable, malicious, deceptive, duplicated, or outside scope. Document significant removals in the research notes and pull request.
